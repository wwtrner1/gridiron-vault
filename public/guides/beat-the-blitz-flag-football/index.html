How to beat the blitz and Cover 0 in flag football

Double slants, quick hitches and one shot over the top: get the ball out before the rusher gets home.

A blitz sends an extra rusher at the quarterback. In flag football, that rusher starts behind the rush line (usually 7 yards off the ball) and gets to the quarterback in about two to three seconds. Cover 0 is the coverage behind it: every other defender in tight man coverage and nobody deep. It's a gamble. Beat it with throws that come out before the rusher arrives, or one shot over the top where no help is coming.

How to spot the blitz before the snap

  • An extra defender walks up to the rush line, or the deep safety creeps down.
  • Defenders press right on top of your receivers with no one in the middle of the field.
  • The rusher leans and stares at the quarterback instead of the receivers.

1. Double slants: beat the rush with timing

Why it works: Against tight man, a slant breaks inside before the defender can react, and the quarterback throws on rhythm, well before the rusher gets home.

Coaching point: No pump fakes. The quarterback catches the snap, looks at Z and throws. If the slant is jammed, the next slant is right behind it.

2. Quick hitches: take what they give

Why it works: Defenders playing off the ball to stop the deep throw leave the short hitch open, and the ball is out in about a second.

Coaching point: Throw to the receiver facing the most open grass. After the catch, the receiver turns upfield right away, because there's nobody behind the first defender.

3. Slant-go: punish Cover 0

Why it works: Cover 0 has no deep help. Once the defender drives on the slant fake, Z is past him with nobody behind him.

Coaching point: Call it after double slants has worked. The quarterback pumps once at the slant to sell it, then throws over the top, early, before the rusher gets home.

Blitz rules for your quarterback

  • Know the hot route. One receiver on every play is the "hot" throw if the rusher comes free: usually the slant or the center sitting in the middle.
  • Get the ball out. Count "one thousand, two thousand" in practice. If he's still holding it at three, the rusher wins.
  • Make them pay once. A completed slant-go against Cover 0 usually ends the blitzing for the day.

Common questions

How do you beat a blitz in flag football?

Throw quickly: slants and hitches come out before the rusher gets home from the rush line. When the defense shows Cover 0 (man with no deep help), one double move like a slant-go can go for a touchdown.

What is Cover 0 in flag football?

Cover 0 is all-out man coverage with no deep safety, usually paired with a rusher or blitz. It stops short throws if the rush gets home, but anything that beats a defender deep is open.

How far back does the rusher start in flag football?

In most leagues, including NFL FLAG 5-on-5, the rusher must start at least 7 yards from the line of scrimmage. Some leagues use a different distance or no rush.
